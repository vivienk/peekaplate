# Non-Voxel-Mesh 3D/2.5D Paradigms for Single Dish Photos: Depth-Driven Relief + Parametric Plate vs. Single-Image 3D Gaussian Splatting

## 1. PeekaPlate's Current T1 2.5D Pipeline vs. WebGL Three.js 2.5D Relief GLB

### Takeaway

PeekaPlate's production T1 pipeline renders a flat DOM/CSS parallax card (`perspective(900px)`, `±3°` rotation, `5px`/`3px` translation) from `≤180 KB` of WebP/SVG layers, with Three.js (`0.182.0`) isolated to the modal AR/camera portal (`portionAR.ts`). Moving to a real WebGL Three.js 2.5D relief `.glb` closes the depth-occlusion, surface-shading, and tabletop-AR silhouette gaps, but requires extending `PublicPortionSchema` (which currently permits only image layers and `≤3°` motion) and updating ADR 0004's `400 KB` T1 payload cap (`checkPortionBundle`) because Three.js + `GLTFLoader` alone consumes `~211–223 KB` gzipped before any GLB bytes.

### Inferences

- **Exact functional gap (CSS T1 card vs. WebGL 2.5D relief `.glb`):**
  1. *Geometric parallax & occlusion:* Current T1 renders a flat 2D quad tilting `±3°` with uniform `5px`/`3px` shift; tall food elements (curled kimchi, stacked toppings) cannot occlude or parallax over the lower plate/broth. A WebGL relief mesh provides genuine Z-depth parallax across `±20°–30°`, surface-normal micro-lighting, and solid bowl/plate thickness in [portionAR.ts](file:///home/yacine105/t3-code-handoff/peekaplate/frontend/diner/src/menu/portionAR.ts#L165-L175) instead of an upright paper-thin `PlaneGeometry`.
  2. *Contract & pipeline gap:* `PublicPortionSchema` (`portion.ts`), `MotionLimits` (`schemas.py`), and `portion_ingestion.py` must be extended to carry a bounded `.glb` descriptor (and `±20°–30°` orbit limits) alongside the T0/2.5D fallback.
  3. *ADR 0004 `400 KB` T1 ceiling conflict:* Because Three.js (`~181–183 KB` gzip) + `GLTFLoader` (`~30–40 KB` gzip) + `MeshoptDecoder` (`~8–10 KB` gzip) + `OrbitControls` already totals `~220–240+ KB` gzipped before Inter `.woff2` fonts and the `.glb` asset itself, a Three.js GLB viewer **cannot** fit inside `checkPortionBundle`'s `400,000`-byte T1 limit (`~180 KB` left after Three.js, before fonts). Deploying it requires either defining a new T1-Relief / T2 budget per ADR 0004's G1 milestone (`dish-3d-delivery.md` §52) or avoiding `GLTFLoader` in T1 via a minimal custom WebGL plane/heightmap shader.

### Gaps

- Exact gzipped byte totals of `frontend/diner`'s compiled `portionRenderer.ts` chunk, `portionAR.ts` (`three@0.182.0`) chunk, and bundled `@fontsource/inter` `.woff2` files were not measured via `vite build` (unsandboxed shell execution timed out awaiting permission).
- Exact gzipped size of `three` `0.183.x` specifically (vs. Bundlephobia's general `~181–183 KB` figure) was not measured directly in the repo.

## 2. Paradigm 1 — Depth-Driven 2.5D Dish Relief Mesh + Parametric Plate Base

### Takeaway

Projecting an unmodified `cutout_rgba.png` directly onto a depth-displaced heightfield mesh backed by a clean procedural ceramic plate/bowl avoids generative food hallucination entirely, preserving crisp sesame seeds, kimchi fibers, and chili-oil highlights while staying under `~500 KB` per mobile GLB (`1024px` WebP + Meshopt). **`microsoft/MoGe-2`** (MIT code/weights, DINOv2 Apache-2.0) is the clear production choice—delivering metric depth, focal length, and sharp surface normals in `<1s` on a T4—whereas **`Depth Anything V2` ViT-B/L/G** (`CC-BY-NC-4.0`), **`apple/ml-depth-pro` / `DepthPro2`** (Apple research-only `LICENSE_MODEL`), and **`Lotus-2`** (`FLUX.1-dev` non-commercial base) are commercially disqualified.

#### Mesh Size, Compression & ADR 0004 Constraints
- Using `gltf-transform` (`--compress meshopt`, `--texture-compress webp`, `--texture-size 1024`, `--simplify-ratio 0.4–0.5`), heightfield meshes compress down to **`40–90 KB`** (`1k–5k` triangles, `1024px` WebP) and **`150–400 KB`** for dense `50k+`-triangle meshes — [Compress-GLB](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQE4k7G-R7TwUr743K-CM9JIuYw5RSj-DsOtzxGLeeubRKvBWYNdpPOPLemSiPlbqNRIgLWHxIesdJpwg8N_12qGyCDOrcfKUpD4TDBkGqEtJ3p8Z60qjKc2cqTiKuGuOcR7cdaXLsP3v8wFfw==).
- Contrast with single-image generative meshes in PeekaPlate's Kaggle benchmarks ([dish-3d-delivery.md](file:///home/yacine105/t3-code-handoff/peekaplate/docs/research/dish-3d-delivery.md#L286-L299)): `TRELLIS.2-4B` (`512`, `remesh=True`, `remesh_project=0.9`) took `369.79s` on dual-T4 (`03_kimchi`) and produced `2,314,564` bytes even at `32,597` mobile faces while introducing `11,059` non-manifold pinch edges on curled cabbage leaves.
- [dish-3d-delivery.md](file:///home/yacine105/t3-code-handoff/peekaplate/docs/research/dish-3d-delivery.md#L71) and [0004-visual-tiers.md](file:///home/yacine105/t3-code-handoff/peekaplate/docs/adr/0004-visual-tiers.md#L17) require preserving captured foreground pixels and prohibiting invented edible surfaces; `MoGe-2`'s official formulation predicts camera-ray 3D point clouds and normals directly aligned to the input image pixels — [arXiv:2507.02546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02546).

### Inferences

- **How direct projective UV mapping + parametric ceramic plate works & why it fits ADR 0004:**
  1. Segmentation (`birefnet-general-lite` / GroundingDINO+SAM) isolates the dish + plate pixels into `cutout_rgba.png`.
  2. `MoGe-2` (`moge-2-vitl-normal`) runs in `<1s` on a single T4 (`<12 GB` VRAM) to produce an image-aligned metric 3D point map `(X(u,v), Y(u,v), Z(u,v))`, camera focal length, and surface normals `(Nx, Ny, Nz)`.
  3. A regular or adaptive foreground-masked grid is displaced along camera rays using smoothed `Z(u,v)`, and each vertex `(u,v)` receives exact projective texture coordinates `(u, 1-v)` sampling the **unmodified** RGB photo (`baseColorTexture`) plus optional surface normals (`normalTexture`). Because no generative decoder re-paints or UV-bakes the food, 100% of crisp kimchi strands, sesame seeds, and chili-oil highlights stay pixel-identical to the captured photo (`0` hallucinated food pixels).
  4. A clean, visibly distinct procedural ceramic plate/bowl underside mesh (lathed from the segmented plate rim or calibration ring) seals the bottom and outer rim as non-edible vessel geometry, and viewer orbit is clamped (`±20°–30°` yaw/pitch) so diners never orbit behind uncaptured back faces.
- **ADR 0004 `publication_eligible` nuance:** Even though the visible food surface is 100% captured photography rather than hallucinated edible geometry, any inferred depth relief and procedural plate underside remain single-view geometric approximations (`1` view cannot prove rear food height or volume). Under ADR 0004, human review (`needs_review`) and explicit disclosure ("depth approximation from single photo", no uncalibrated physical-size claims) are still required, though the risk of misleading diners with hallucinated ingredients is drastically lower than `TripoSR`/`TRELLIS`.
- **Texture resolution vs. `<500 KB` mobile GLB budget:** A `2048×2048` WebP texture on high-frequency dishes like `03_kimchi` (`2000×1125` native) risks pushing a GLB above `400–500 KB` on its own (for context, `portion_ingestion.py` drops down to `640px` WebP at quality `85–65` to fit `180 KB`, and `dish-3d-delivery.md` shows `512×512` `sd-turbo/cutout_rgba.png` was `395 KB` PNG). Using `1024×1024`–`1280×1280` WebP (matching T0 `detail-1280`) + `Meshopt` geometry keeps typical relief GLBs cleanly under `500 KB` (often `150–350 KB`), while `2048×2048` can serve as an optional desktop/high-DPI variant.
- **Visual failure modes:**
  - *No 360° spin:* Cannot inspect the unseen rear edge of tall plates/bowls; strict orbit clamping (`±20°–30°`) is mandatory.
  - *Grazing-angle stretching ("rubber-sheeting"):* Steep vertical transitions (e.g., tall stacked kimchi leaves or a deep bowl wall viewed from ~45°) stretch few image-plane pixels across tall Z-triangles unless depth gradients are clamped/smoothed, silhouette triangles are extruded cleanly into the parametric plate bed, and edge alpha is feathered.
  - *90° overhead shots (`01_pizza`):* Very low-relief overhead dishes produce subtle shallow elevation (`Z` ~1–3 cm) — truthful to the camera angle, whereas generative models invent fake puffy crusts.

### Gaps

## 3. Paradigm 2 — Single-Image 3D Gaussian Splatting (`apple/ml-sharp`, `TRELLIS-image-large` 3DGS, `Flash3D`, `Splatter Image`, `LGM`)

### Takeaway

Single-image 3D Gaussian Splatting is **not viable** for PeekaPlate production under ADR 0004: `apple/ml-sharp` (`~1.18M` Gaussians, `~63 MB` `.ply`), `Flash3D` (`CC BY-NC-SA 4.0` + `UniDepth` `CC-BY-NC 4.0`), and `microsoft/TRELLIS-image-large`'s 3DGS/rendering stack all carry **non-commercial licensing blockers**, while `TRELLIS` and `LGM` still hallucinate unseen food surfaces. Furthermore, even with `.spz` (`~10×`) or `.sog` (`~15–20×`) compression, dish payloads (`~1.5–15 MB`) and Three.js splat engines (`@sparkjsdev/spark` v2 at `~5 MiB` uncompressed / `~11×` Three.js gzipped) exceed ADR 0004's `400 KB` T1 budget by roughly an order of magnitude.

### Inferences

- **`TRELLIS-image-large` 3DGS vs. `TRELLIS.2-4B` O-Voxel meshes for `03_kimchi`:** While `TRELLIS-image-large`'s `200k–600k` alpha-blended 3D Gaussians naturally soften wispy/wet shredded kimchi margins compared to `TRELLIS.2`'s `34k`-face manifold triangles (which pinched into `11,059` non-manifold edges in V31), `TRELLIS-image-large` still hallucinates unseen back/side food surfaces identically to `TRELLIS.2-4B` (failing ADR 0004's anti-fabrication rule), triggers `diff-gaussian-rasterization`/`mip-splatting` commercial-use restrictions, and outputs `15–60 MB` `.ply` files (`~1.5–4 MB` even after ~10–15× compression).
- **Why `ml-sharp` cannot replace Depth-Relief `.glb` in PeekaPlate:** Despite sub-second A100 synthesis and sharp nearby parallax (`±15–30°`), `ml-sharp` is legally blocked by Apple's `LICENSE_MODEL`, requires `~4–6+ MB` per dish even after 10×–15× `.spz`/`.sog` compression of its `63 MB` / `~1.18M`-Gaussian `.ply`, and forces heavy client splat-sorting JS/WASM (`@sparkjsdev/spark` v2 alone exceeds ADR 0004's entire `400 KB` T1 limit by `~5×`).
- **Overall Architecture Verdict:** Paradigm 1 (`microsoft/MoGe-2` projective depth/normal relief + unmodified `cutout_rgba.png` + procedural ceramic plate `.glb` + `±20°–30°` orbit clamp) is the only single-photo paradigm here that satisfies **all three** of commercial licensing (`MIT`), zero edible-surface hallucination (ADR 0004), and sub-500 KB mobile `.glb` delivery (`Meshopt` + `1024px` WebP), whereas single-image 3DGS fails on licensing, payload size, and/or backside hallucination.

### Gaps

- Exact `.spz` and `.sog` compressed file sizes specifically from `apple/ml-sharp` (`~63 MB` `.ply`, SH0) and `TRELLIS-image-large` (`200k–600k` splats) on `03_kimchi` and `01_pizza` were not empirically benchmarked (cited `~15.3 MB` `.sog` / `~25.8 MB` `.spz` numbers come from a 1M-splat SH3 benchmark).
- Exact minified+gzipped byte counts for `@mkkellogg/gaussian-splats-3d` and `@sparkjsdev/spark` v2.x were not printed directly by search results (only uncompressed `~5 MiB` / `~578.6 KB` and `~11×` Three.js gzipped ratio were observed).
- Zero-shot visual quality of `szymanowiczs/splatter-image` (trained on ShapeNet/CO3D/Objaverse) on plated food like `03_kimchi` or `01_pizza` was not found in published benchmarks.
